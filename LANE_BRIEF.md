## What is known (measured 2026-09-13 on the build host, perry origin/main + three unrelated fixes, `--no-auto-optimize`)
Per operation, perry vs bun: `new RegExp(<14,116-char emoji-regex source>, "g")` 631 µs vs 2.3 µs (274×); `.test("a")` on a reused emoji regex 11.1 µs vs 0.4 µs; `.test("🚀")` 12.6 vs 0.3 µs; `strip-ansi` (`replace` with a module-level `ansiRegex`) 14.3 µs vs 0.1 µs (143×); `/^\p{Default_Ignorable_Code_Point}$/u.test("a")` 1.9 vs 0.2 µs; `Intl.Segmenter` over a 90-char line 68 vs 12 µs; `eastAsianWidth()` 10×. `string-width` 7 constructs `emojiRegex()` once **per grapheme** (inside its loop), so 9,000 `stringWidth()` calls over three help lines take 340 s under perry vs 72 ms under bun, and that is the whole cost of `opencode --help` (31 s user) and `run --help` (20 s).

An earlier analysis of the regex subsystem (2026-09-05, not in the repo) concluded: the matching automaton is ~2.3× node per byte (fine); the cost is (1) per-call wrapper overhead 20–40× node, (2) eager compile at construction 12–22× node, (3) the construction cache `REGEX_CACHE` (512 entries, whole-map `clear()` on overflow) SipHashes the entire pattern text on every construction/lookup, and patterns the standard engine rejects get compiled by the fallback engine and only a placeholder in the cache. It measured "construct a cached 11 KB emoji pattern: 5 µs node vs 75 µs perry" — today's 631 µs for the 14 KB emoji-regex@10.6.0 pattern suggests this pattern is not being served from the cache at all (fallback-engine path, cache eviction, or cache miss on the hash). Establish which before changing anything.
